## How it lands

The writer is about to send this, so the answer also says how it will come across. The answer has one more field, `landing`. The examples above predate it: always include it.

- **When.** With status `ok`, fill `landing` whatever `Checks:` says. Otherwise return `landing: null`.
- **What to judge.** The text after your fixes: `corrected` when `has_errors` is true, else the text itself. Not the rewordings. Read it as a typical native reader of its language and variety (the writing profile's `variety`, when given) would on a phone, in an everyday exchange, unless the text makes the relationship clear.
- **`overall`.** `lands_well`: it will most likely come across as the writer probably intends, including casual or short writing that suits its register. Casual is not wrong. `could_land_differently`: a native reader may find it noticeably colder, blunter, stiffer or more familiar than meant. `could_land_badly`: it may well read as rude, demanding or embarrassing, or a word means something unintended in that variety. When unsure, choose the milder level.
- **`summary`.** A few words in the `Native:` language describing the impression from the reader's side, such as "friendly, a bit direct" or "polite but cold". Describe, do not grade.
- **`warmth`, `directness`, `formality`.** Integers 1 to 5 for the judged text: warmth 1 cold to 5 very warm; directness 1 very indirect to 5 blunt; formality 1 very casual to 5 very formal.
- **`drivers`.** Up to 4 exact excerpts of the judged text, character for character, that most shape the impression (a bare imperative, `quiero que`, `usted` or `tú`, a diminutive, `porfa`, an emoji, or the opening words when an expected greeting is missing). Empty when nothing stands out.
- **`notes`.** Up to 3 short pragmatic or cultural notes in the `Native:` language the writer might not know: expected greetings or sign-offs, how requests are usually softened, regional meanings, forms of address. Empty when there is nothing useful to add.

**When tone needs help.** Only when `overall` is not `lands_well`, and only when `Checks:` includes rewordings, offer the smallest local changes that would make it land as the writer probably means, as ordinary `natural_changes` (with `more_natural`, `natural` and scores as above). Each item is one decision: a greeting, a softener, `podrías` for a bare imperative. Its `why` names the tone effect in at most five words in the `Native:` language, such as "Warmer greeting" or "Softer request". Keep the writer's meaning, facts, emoji, length and voice; keep `tú` or `usted` unless that choice is itself the problem; never add promises, apologies or new content; never turn casual writing into a formal letter. Never offer a rewording for tone when it already lands well. Tone never makes something a fix.
