# Type to accept

Accepting a change by typing it. A switch in **Settings › Checking**, off by default.

## The problem

Accepting a change is one tap. The text gets better, but the writer doesn't: a tap on
Accept doesn't make them notice that *estas* became *estàs*, so they write *estas* again
tomorrow. The owner wanted a way to make the correction pass through their own fingers.

## How it works for the writer

With **Type to accept** on, every check card (the popup over other apps, the selection-menu
card, Chat's review panel, Notes, the clipboard card: they all share `ResultCard`) works
like this:

- The [review flow](review-flow.md) still opens on the first change and walks them in
  order. On the open change, the **Accept** button is replaced by a field. The field takes
  focus and the keyboard opens.
- The writer types the change's new text, the bold part of "estas → **estàs**".
- The field compares as they type. While the text is still the start of the change,
  nothing happens. The moment one character is off, the field turns to the error state
  (the theme's error colour and outline) with a short "Not quite" under it, short enough
  to sit beside Skip on a phone. Screen readers hear "Not quite. Check the change above".
  Fixing the character clears it.
- As soon as the text matches exactly, it is **correct** (below): a short beat that shows
  it, then the change is accepted, with no extra tap. It is the same Accept as the
  button's: the same History recording, the same move to the next change, whose field
  opens empty and keeps the keyboard up.
- **Skip** stays beside the field, as does the Phrasebook star.
- **Teach me** closes the keyboard and leaves the field, so the lesson has the room. What
  was typed stays; a tap on the field brings the keyboard back.
- With the switch off, the card is exactly as before.

## Correct: the beat before the accept

Accepting the moment the last character landed made the field vanish under the writer's
finger, which "feels like a bug". Now an exact match first shows that it's right:

- **The field.** It locks (more typing is ignored), its outline turns ink, its text turns
  semibold and a check appears at its end. Screen readers hear "Correct" (the check is a
  polite live region). The theme has one colour, proof vermilion, kept for marks and
  errors; success is ink with a check, as elsewhere on the card ("Looks good", "All
  accepted"). No overlay, no banner.
- **The text above.** The change resolves in place: its underline fades and the new words
  get a brief soft highlight in the mark's colour, which rises, holds and fades
  (`SETTLE_MILLIS`, 600 ms: twice the expand time plus the collapse time of the
  [expand pattern](expand-pattern.md)), so the writer sees their sentence fixed. The card's
  text already shows the new words, so for a replacement there are no struck-through old
  words to remove; deletions keep Accept.
- **The hold.** `CORRECT_HOLD_MILLIS` is 700 ms from the match: under about 400 ms the
  success isn't registered before it's gone; over a second it feels sluggish across many
  changes. Then the change is accepted through the same `onAccept`, exactly once, and the
  card moves on with the existing motion. After the last change it's the same beat, then
  the card's usual finished state ("2 changes accepted, Undo, Replace").
- **Leaving during the hold** (the card closes, Edit message, Skip, rotation) still accepts
  the matched change, once: the writer typed it right.
- **Motion.** The hold and the other waits are counted in frames, like animations, so they
  pause with the screen. The system's animator scale shortens the motion (the settle, the
  panels), not the hold, so the success state always shows; with animations off the change
  simply shows settled.
- **Feel and sound.** Two switches under Type to accept, shown while it is on: **Vibrate
  when correct** (on by default), a light `CONFIRM` haptic (a keyboard tap where that isn't
  available), not played when the system's touch feedback is off; and **Sound when
  correct** (off by default), a short, quiet two-note sound on the sonification stream at
  its system volume, never while the ringer is on silent or vibrate. Both are stored in
  `lab_type_to_accept`. The sound is original, generated by `scripts/make_correct_sound.py`
  (see `assets/lab_sounds_SOURCES.md`), 11 KB.

## Room to type

The owner, typing a change in Chat with the keyboard up: "I can't see the actual thing I'm
correcting." The screen showed the partner's last bubble, Edit message, and the card
scrolled to its explanation; the writer's own text, with the change in it, had scrolled
away. The fix is about size, not a different mode: while the field has focus and the
keyboard is up, the writer's text around the change and the field are in view together,
and the space comes from what can give it up.

- **The text around the change.** A text of more than four lines narrows to the change's
  lines with one line above and one below (`typingWindow`), faded at the edges where it
  goes on. The rest of the text is still there for screen readers; it widens again when
  typing ends. A short text shows whole.
- **In view together.** The card (or the screen, in Notes) scrolls so that the line before
  the change, the change line ("de una → d'una"), its reason and the field are all in view;
  if that can't fit, at least the change itself and the field. It does this once the panel
  has opened, and again whenever the keyboard settles at a new height. While typing, the
  explanation's own scroll into view (which brought the whole panel up and pushed the text
  away) stands back.
- **Nothing is removed.** Impact and Confidence, Teach me, Accept all and Meaning stay on
  the card, below the field, where scrolling reaches them.
- **Chat.** The review normally takes at most 62% of the height above the keyboard, so the
  conversation stays in view. While typing it takes all of it: the conversation yields its
  height (it stays in place, scrolled, and comes back as the keyboard closes). Edit message
  stays above the card as a slim bar, and Send as written keeps its place under it.
- **The floating card** normally leaves a fifth of the screen free to tap away; while
  typing it takes the room above the keyboard. **Notes** ends the page above the keyboard
  while typing, so scrolling can reach the field.
- **When it ends.** As soon as the field loses focus or the keyboard closes (after the last
  change, Back, Teach me), the layout relaxes, with the expand pattern's motion. The height
  shares animate, not the heights, so the review still follows the keyboard as it moves.
- **How it's known.** The field's real focus and the keyboard's real IME insets
  (`imeVisible()`, the signal Back already uses), held for 250 ms when they drop
  (`TYPING_HANDOFF_MILLIS`) so moving from one change to the next doesn't make the layout
  jump. With the switch off there is no field, so nothing changes.

## Decisions

**What to type.** The change's new text as the card shows it: line breaks inside it read
as one space, and spaces at either end are dropped. Spaces inside count ("de la", not
"dela").

**Exact characters.** Case, accents and punctuation all count: accents are often the
lesson, so *estas* and *estás* are both "not quite" for *estàs*. Two exceptions, both
about how text is encoded rather than what it says:

- *Unicode forms are the same.* é typed as one character, or as e followed by a combining
  accent, is the same é (canonical equivalence, compared in decomposed form). The writer
  isn't told they're off while a letter still waits for its accent (a dead key).
- *Apostrophes are one.* ' (straight), ’ (typographic) and ʼ (modifier letter) all
  match each other. The model writes either, and keyboards type either; making the writer
  hunt for the right one teaches nothing about the language.

**Deletions and spacing keep Accept.** A deletion ("remove yo") has nothing to type, and a
spacing change (a space, a line break) has nothing visible to type. Asking the writer to
type nothing, or a lone space, would be a ritual, not a lesson. So those changes keep the
normal Accept button. An insertion is typed like any other change.

**Long rewordings are typed in full.** A rewording can be a whole clause. The field wraps,
so the writer sees everything they typed, and typed line breaks are dropped. Typing a long
rewording is the point of the setting; Skip and Accept all are there when it isn't worth it.

**What still accepts without typing.** Accept all, under each section, and the screen-reader
action on the text that accepts a change ("Accept amigo to amigos") stay as they are.
Accept all is a deliberate choice to take a whole section at once, and the screen-reader
action is how the card is used without seeing the field. The setting changes the one
tap that happens on every change, not every way to accept.

**Where the switch lives.** Settings › Checking, under What to suggest: both say how checks
behave on the card. Not App, which is about the app's shell (Start on).

## Accessibility

- The field's label names what to type: "Type estàs to accept it". The placeholder says
  "Type the change".
- The error is set on the field (Android's error state) and its message is a polite live
  region, so it is read out as it appears, in full ("Not quite. Check the change above").
- A match is announced as "Correct" (a polite live region on the field's check).
- While the text is narrowed for typing, screen readers still get the whole text and its
  per-change actions; nothing on the card leaves the semantics tree.
- The review flow's spoken position ("Change 2 of 5. estas becomes estàs") is unchanged.

## How it's built

- `data/lab/TypeToAcceptPreferences.kt`: the switch, in its own preferences file
  (`lab_type_to_accept`), never the app's `settings`. Nothing changes in History.
- `ui/settings/TypeToAcceptSetting.kt`: the switch row (`SwitchRow`), placed in the
  Checking card of `SettingsScreen`.
- `ui/card/TypeToAccept.kt`: `TypeToAccept` (the pure rules: `target`, `typed`,
  `progress`), and `TypeToAcceptField`, built on the shared `LinguizeTextField`, which
  accepts through the same `onAccept` as the button, once.
- Small hooks: `Suggestions` reads the switch once per check and passes it through the
  review flow (`ReviewSelection.typeToAccept`). `ChangeExplanation` turns it into a
  `TypeTarget` (the change's id and text) for the open change; `ExplanationPanel` shows the
  field instead of Accept when it has one. The field is keyed on the change's id, so each
  change starts empty. `LinguizeTextField` gained `announceSupporting` for the live message.
- Room to type: `CardTyping` (in `TypeToAccept.kt`) holds whether a field has focus with the
  keyboard up (`active`), the change resolving (`resolving`) and where the field sits.
  `ResultCard` keeps one, or uses its host's (`LocalCardTyping`): Chat's
  `ConversationAndReview`, `FloatingPopup` and Notes' `NotePage` provide one so they can
  give the card the room. `TrackTyping` sets `active`. `HighlightedText` narrows the text
  (`typingWindow`, a layout modifier with a fade) and keeps the change and the field in view
  (a `BringIntoViewRequester` with the rectangle from the change's line to the field).
- Correct: `TypeToAcceptField` is one field per explanation (its state is keyed on the
  change, the field isn't, so focus and the keyboard stay between changes). It locks on a
  match, waits `CORRECT_HOLD_MILLIS` by the frame clock (`frameDelay`) and accepts once,
  also from its dispose. `LinguizeTextField` gained `success` (ink outline, check,
  "Correct") and `spokenSupporting`. `CorrectFeedback.kt`: `CorrectFeedback` reads the two
  switches and calls a `FeedbackOutput` (the device, or `LocalFeedbackOutput` in tests).
  `TeachMe` takes `onOpen`; `LocalLessonTeacher` lets tests give the card a lesson writer.

Tests: `TypeToAcceptTest` (the rules; the switches gate the feedback; the settle fits in the
hold), `TypeToAcceptCardTest` (off is unchanged; a deletion keeps Accept; typing, the error
and its clearing, accepting and moving on; Skip), `TypeToAcceptLayoutTest` (real IME insets
and its own frame clock: the change and the field in the card's viewport together, in a
long text and in a lower section; the rest stays below the field; the layout relaxes when
the keyboard closes or focus leaves; off is unchanged; Correct, then one accept after the
hold, with focus kept into the next change; leaving mid-hold accepts once; the switches;
Teach me leaves the field and keeps what was typed), `ChatTypingRoomTest` (the review takes
the height and the conversation yields it, then gets it back), `TypeToAcceptSettingTest`
(off by default, persists; the two switches under it, their defaults, persisting).

## Limits

- The switch is read when a check is shown. Turning it on while a card is open applies from
  the next check.
- The field opening the keyboard by itself, the room to type, the haptic and the sound
  have been checked in tests (Robolectric, with IME insets sent to the view), not yet on a
  device: not on the popup over other apps, where the keyboard and the card share the
  screen, and not with each keyboard's own animation.
- Moving from a fix to a rewording changes passage, so a new field takes focus a frame
  later and the keyboard may blink there. Within a passage the field stays.
- The sound loads when a card first shows with it on, so the very first match after turning
  it on in Settings may be silent.
- Keyboard autocorrect is asked to stay off in the field; some keyboards ignore that.
